## Purpose

One page per service, for a visitor who is deciding. It answers four questions: what do I receive, how does it unfold, what is not included, and what comes after. The studio's checklists are its method, so phase titles are always visible and their items sit behind a disclosure button rather than a wall of text.

## Layout (top to bottom)

1. `PublicLayout` header.
2. `PageHeader`: breadcrumb Services / <service>, h1 service name, subtitle = primary outcome, ladder word `Badge`.
3. Identity card (raised): "Ideal for", "The question it answers" (01, 02, 04) and / or "Our promise" (03, E).
4. **What you receive**: `deliveryContents` as a list.
5. **How it unfolds**: one card per phase / stage - "Step n", the phase title, a "What it covers" button (`aria-expanded` / `aria-controls`) that reveals the checklist items, plus the phase notes when the playbook has them ("Once payment is confirmed, administration activates the project.").
6. **What is not included** (only service 01 declares it today).
7. **The natural next step** (02 and 03): the playbook sentence as a pull quote.
8. CTA card "Tell us about your space" with "Start with this service" -> `/start?service=<code>`.
9. Previous / next service pager.
10. `PublicLayout` footer.

## Logic

- `:slug` is matched against `SERVICES[].slug`. An unknown slug renders an `EmptyState` with a link back to `/services` rather than redirecting, so a stale link explains itself.
- Grouped phases (01 stage 2 "USER / SPACE / AESTHETIC DIRECTION / EXPECTATION", 01 stage 4, 02 and 03 equivalents) are flattened with `phaseItems()` so the disclosure shows one readable checklist.
- Phase ids (`01-1`, `03-11`, `E-6`) are used only as DOM ids for `aria-controls`; the visible number is the position in the list.
- `?service=<code>` carries the service into the intake form; `startHref()` accepts a code or a slug (so voice and WebMCP can pass either).
